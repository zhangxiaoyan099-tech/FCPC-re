\section{Properties and Optimization Interpretation of FCPC}

This appendix replaces the previous convergence argument with results that
match the implemented mechanism. Local differential privacy (LDP) is applied
only to the label histogram and sample-count metadata used for pairing; it is
not applied to stochastic gradients. We therefore do not introduce an LDP
gradient-variance term. Moreover, JSDN determines the matching and is not
assumed to be linearly related to gradient dissimilarity. The relationship
between JSDN and observed gradient divergence is evaluated empirically in the
revised experiments.

\subsection{Definition and Basic Properties of JSDN}

Let $\bm{p}_i,\bm{p}_j\in\Delta^{C-1}$ denote the label distributions of two
clients, and let $N_i,N_j>0$ denote their sample counts. With logarithms in
base two, define
\begin{align}
 \JSD(\bm{p}_i,\bm{p}_j)
 &=\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bm{p}_i\Vert\bm{m}_{ij})
   +\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bm{p}_j\Vert\bm{m}_{ij}),\\
 \bm{m}_{ij}&=\frac{\bm{p}_i+\bm{p}_j}{2},\\
 q_{ij}&=\frac{|N_i-N_j|}{N_i+N_j},\\
 \JSDN_{ij}&=(1-\lambda)\JSD(\bm{p}_i,\bm{p}_j)+\lambda q_{ij},
 \qquad \lambda\in[0,1].
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Boundedness and symmetry]
For all valid client pairs, $\JSDN_{ij}=\JSDN_{ji}$ and
$0\leq \JSDN_{ij}\leq 1$. Furthermore, $\JSDN_{ij}=0$ if the two clients have
identical label distributions and equal sample counts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Jensen--Shannon divergence with base-two logarithms is symmetric and lies
in $[0,1]$. The normalized quantity difference $q_{ij}$ is also symmetric and
lies in $[0,1]$ because $|N_i-N_j|\leq N_i+N_j$. JSDN is a convex combination
of these two quantities, so it is symmetric and belongs to $[0,1]$. If both
metadata components are identical, both terms are zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Single-skew reduction]
If $N_i=N_j$ for all candidate pairs, ranking pairs by JSDN is identical to
ranking them by JSD whenever $\lambda<1$. If
$\bm{p}_i=\bm{p}_j$ for all candidate pairs, ranking pairs by JSDN is
identical to ranking them by $q_{ij}$ whenever $\lambda>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under equal sample counts, $q_{ij}=0$ and
$\JSDN_{ij}=(1-\lambda)\JSD_{ij}$; multiplication by the positive constant
$1-\lambda$ preserves the ranking. Under equal label distributions,
$\JSD_{ij}=0$ and $\JSDN_{ij}=\lambda q_{ij}$, for which the same argument
applies.
\end{proof}

These propositions justify the use of a fixed $\lambda$ at the two
single-skew limits, but they do not identify a universally optimal value in
mixed-skew settings. Consequently, the revised manuscript treats
$\lambda=0.3$ as an empirically selected default and reports sensitivity over
heterogeneity levels and client scales instead of claiming a theoretical
optimum.

\subsection{Optimization of the Paired Local Objective}

During a local update, client $i$ treats the paired client's previous-round
model $\bm{r}_i^t=\bm{w}_{\pi_t(i)}^{t-1}$ as fixed and minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_i^t(\bm{w})=F_i(\bm{w})+
 \beta\|\bm{w}-\bm{r}_i^t\|_2^2,\qquad \beta\geq0.
\end{equation}
For the first communication round, the implementation defines the unavailable
previous-round reference as the common initialization $\bm{w}^0$. All partner
references are snapshotted before any client begins the current round, which
prevents an order-dependent asymmetric update in sequential simulation.

\begin{proposition}[Local descent and stationarity]
Assume $F_i$ is differentiable, bounded below, and $L$-smooth. Then
$\Phi_i^t$ is $(L+2\beta)$-smooth. For the update
\begin{equation}
 \bm{w}^{s+1}=\bm{w}^{s}-\eta\nabla\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})
\end{equation}
with $0<\eta\leq1/(L+2\beta)$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s+1})
 \leq \Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})
 -\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
After $S$ local steps,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{0\leq s<S}\|\nabla\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})\|_2^2
 \leq
 \frac{2\left[\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{0})-\inf_{\bm{w}}\Phi_i^t(\bm{w})\right]}
 {\eta S}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The gradient of the quadratic term is
$2\beta(\bm{w}-\bm{r}_i^t)$ and is $2\beta$-Lipschitz. Hence
$\nabla\Phi_i^t$ is $(L+2\beta)$-Lipschitz. Applying the descent lemma with
$L_\Phi=L+2\beta$ gives
\begin{align}
 \Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s+1})
 &\leq \Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})
 -\eta\left(1-\frac{L_\Phi\eta}{2}\right)
 \|\nabla\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})\|_2^2\\
 &\leq \Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})
 -\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\Phi_i^t(\bm{w}^{s})\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Summing over $s=0,\ldots,S-1$, using the lower bound of $\Phi_i^t$, and
upper-bounding the minimum by the average proves the final inequality.
\end{proof}

This result establishes that the implemented quadratic paired objective has a
standard nonconvex stationarity guarantee. It deliberately does not claim
that larger JSDN causes a faster global convergence rate; that causal claim
would require assumptions connecting metadata distributions to model
gradients and is instead tested through controlled pairing ablations.

\section{Matching Guarantee and Odd-Client Handling}

For a participating client set $V_t$, construct the complete undirected graph
$G_t=(V_t,E_t)$ with nonnegative edge weight
$d_{ij}=\JSDN_{ij}$. FCPC greedily scans edges in nonincreasing weight order
and accepts an edge if neither endpoint has already been matched.

\begin{theorem}[Greedy matching approximation]
Let $M_{\mathrm{g}}$ be the greedy dissimilarity matching and
$M^\star$ a maximum-weight matching. For nonnegative JSDN weights,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in M_{\mathrm{g}}}d_e
 \geq \frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in M^\star}d_e.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider a greedy edge $e=(i,j)$ when it is selected. At most two edges of
$M^\star$ are incident to $i$ or $j$. Any such optimal edge still available
at this step has weight no larger than $d_e$, because the greedy scan is in
nonincreasing order. Charge those at most two optimal edges to $e$. Every
optimal edge is charged once, and the total charge to each greedy edge is at
most $2d_e$. Thus $w(M^\star)\leq2w(M_{\mathrm{g}})$.
\end{proof}

When $|V_t|$ is odd, one client is necessarily unmatched by any ordinary
matching. The revised FCPC procedure does \emph{not} discard this client: it
still performs local training and contributes to global aggregation, but does
not receive the paired regularizer in that round. A fair-bye rule chooses a
client with the smallest previous unmatched count before greedily matching the
remaining clients. For an always-participating odd client set, the unmatched
counts of any two clients therefore differ by at most one. This preserves the
training participation rate and prevents one representative client from being
systematically excluded from regularization.

\section{Complexity and Communication Accounting}

For $n=|V_t|$ participating clients, $C$ classes, and a model state containing
$P_b$ bytes:

\begin{itemize}
 \item Building the dense JSDN matrix costs $O(n^2C)$ arithmetic and
 $O(n^2)$ storage. The implementation normalizes each histogram once and
 evaluates the matrix in bounded-memory vectorized blocks.
 \item Sorting the $n(n-1)/2$ edges and scanning them for greedy matching costs
 $O(n^2\log n)$ time and $O(n^2)$ storage. Exact maximum-weight matching is
 used only as an ablation because its worst-case cost is cubic.
 \item Computing the FCPC penalty is $O(P)$ arithmetic over trainable
 parameters, where $P$ is the parameter count, and requires one frozen partner
 state.
 \item Relative to FedAvg, FCPC does not add server communication rounds.
 However, a full-model implementation adds $2|M_t|P_b$ partner-upload bytes
 per round, in addition to $nP_b$ server downloads and $nP_b$ server uploads.
 With an even participating set, the model-payload total is therefore
 $3nP_b$ for FCPC versus $2nP_b$ for FedAvg---a 50\% increase in total model
 traffic, or one additional model upload per client. This cost is measured and
 reported rather than described as negligible.
\end{itemize}

The revised code logs JSDN construction and pairing time, round time, process
CPU utilization, peak resident memory, GPU utilization, peak allocated GPU
memory, per-link model bytes, and cumulative traffic. Scalability results for
10--1000 clients and accuracy--communication trade-offs are reported in the
revised experimental section.

\section{Threat Model}

The main statistical-heterogeneity analysis assumes honest-but-curious
clients and an authenticated server. LDP protects the uploaded metadata but
does not make FCPC Byzantine robust. In particular, a malicious partner could
supply a poisoned reference model. Recent ScaleSign and MSGuard results show
that model-poisoning attacks and multi-strategy defenses require update-level
robustness mechanisms \cite{yang2025scalesign}. Because FCPC does not modify
server aggregation, it can be combined with robust aggregation or detection,
but such a composition must filter both server-bound and partner-bound model
states. The revised manuscript states this limitation explicitly and does not
claim adversarial robustness from dissimilarity-aware pairing alone.
